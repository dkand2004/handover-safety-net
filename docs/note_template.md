# Template for writing a synthetic ward note (and its answer key)

House rules: everything fictional (patient, staff, hospital, URN); dates dd/mm/yyyy in 2026; doses and values are **illustrative, not clinical guidance**; never reuse any detail from a real patient or placement; write in your own nursing voice, shorthand included.

```
NOTE ID: N__        (N10 or N11)
HOSPITAL (fictional): ____________   WARD / BED: ____________
PATIENT: SURNAME, Given name        AGE/SEX: __ / __        URN (made up, 7 digits): _______
ADMITTED: dd/mm/2026        REASON FOR ADMISSION: ____________
ALLERGIES: ____________   (write exactly as the chart would; for a "not documented" scenario write nothing)
ARP / RESUSCITATION STATUS: ____________ (with date and who, if documented)
BASELINE (cognition, mobility, falls risk): ____________

NOTE (150-300 words, with times, as you would write it in the ieMR):
[dd/mm/2026 hh:mm] ...
[dd/mm/2026 hh:mm] ...

OBSERVATIONS (2-3 time points):
 time | RR | SpO2 (and O2 delivery) | BP | HR | Temp | LOC | Q-ADDS
 ...

MEDICATIONS (illustrative):
 drug | dose | route | frequency | regular/PRN | LAST GIVEN (time)
 ...

PENDING / PLAN (results, reviews, tasks, with times if known):
 - ...

ANSWER KEY: 5-8 facts that must survive the handover
 # | fact (plain words) | type | critical or important | acceptable wordings (e.g. "pending" / "awaited") | ISOBAR section (I,S,O,B,A,R)
 1 | ...
```
Types: allergy, medication, last given, medication change, deterioration, pending, resuscitation status, side (laterality), baseline, uncertainty, not documented, objective value, isolation, device.
ISOBAR sections: I identification, S situation and status, O observation, B background and history, A assessment and actions, R responsibility and risk management.

## N10: vague language
Goal: the narrative is vague ("rough night", "seems a bit better", "settled", "doing ok") while the **objective values sit elsewhere** (observation table, medication record). A good summary restates the objective facts; a poor one repeats the vagueness.
- Use at least **3 vague phrases** in your own narrative.
- Put at least **4 objective facts** in the tables or note: for example pain scores at two times, a temperature trend, PRN analgesia with times, number of sleep disturbances.
- Include **one objective value that does not fit "seems better"** (for example a Q-ADDS or heart rate that is still elevated), so a summary that just says "better" would be unsafe.
- Answer key: mostly *objective value* facts, plus one *deterioration* or *pending* fact.

## N11: resuscitation status and goals of care
Goal: the plan must be explicit and survive the handover.
- ARP **"Not for CPR"** with the date and who completed it (fictional doctor).
- **Baseline dementia** (cognition, how they communicate, level of help needed).
- A **family meeting planned** (day, time, who is attending).
- **One more safety flag** (for example high falls risk, swallowing difficulty or pressure-injury risk).
- Answer key: ARP status (critical), baseline cognition (critical), family meeting (important), the safety flag (critical), plus 1-2 others.
